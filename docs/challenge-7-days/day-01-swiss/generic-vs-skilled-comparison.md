# Generic vs Skilled Comparison

Status: PARTIAL

## Generic Legal-AI Output

A generic brief tends to:

- lead with broad fairness language;
- narrate the dispute before establishing admissibility;
- cite plausible but unverified provisions;
- blur facts, allegations and inferences;
- invoke the right to be heard without showing which lower-court consideration is attacked;
- use confident language even when source verification is incomplete.

Risk: this style can look persuasive but fail under Art. 42 BGG and Art. 106 Abs. 2 BGG because it does not engage precisely with the lower-court reasoning.

## Swiss-Federal-Court-Style Output

The skill output should:

- identify the challenged decision immediately;
- establish the federal procedural route first;
- keep facts compact and record-based;
- separate admissibility, facts and grievances;
- target each grievance at a specific consideration;
- avoid unverified authorities;
- abstain when only metadata or secondary sources are available;
- request a precise procedural outcome.

## Concrete Difference

Generic:

`The decision is unfair because the court ignored important documents and violated the mother's rights.`

Skilled:

`Die Vorinstanz hat die mit der Berufung bezeichneten Lohnbelege nicht in die Unterhaltsberechnung einbezogen und nicht begründet, weshalb sie dennoch von unverändertem Einkommen ausgeht. Die Rüge betrifft einen entscheidwesentlichen Punkt.`

## Why The Skilled Version Is Better

The skilled version:

- avoids moral rhetoric;
- identifies the procedural defect;
- links the point to the lower-court reasoning;
- makes clear why the issue matters for the dispositive result;
- remains compatible with the observed institutional style.

## Limitation

This comparison is based on a small official corpus. It is reliable as a drafting discipline checklist, not as a comprehensive model of Swiss Federal Supreme Court writing.
