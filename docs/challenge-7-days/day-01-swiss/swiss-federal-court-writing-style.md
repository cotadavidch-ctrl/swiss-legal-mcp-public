# Skill: swiss-federal-court-writing-style

Status: PARTIAL

Purpose: Assist private-beta drafting of Swiss Federal Supreme Court style submissions in German, using a cautious institutional model derived from verified official decisions of the II. civil-law division. This is not legal advice and not a substitute for official source verification or qualified legal review.

## Scope

Supported with MEDIUM-HIGH confidence:

- German-language civil-law federal appeals.
- Beschwerde in Zivilsachen and related simplified civil proceedings.
- Institutional structure and tone of the II. zivilrechtliche Abteilung.

Not supported:

- Individual judge imitation.
- Authorship attribution.
- Public filing-ready briefs without human legal review.
- French or Italian style replication as a primary model.
- Unverified citations, sources or facts.

## Required Source Discipline

Before using a citation:

1. Resolve the case through an official source.
2. Confirm identity, date, court, division and full-text status.
3. Do not rely on metadata-only records for legal propositions.
4. Use Fedlex for statutes.
5. If a case or statute cannot be verified, omit it or mark it as unverified.

## German Drafting Pattern

Preferred structure:

1. `An das Schweizerische Bundesgericht`
2. Parties and challenged decision.
3. Requests for relief.
4. Admissibility.
5. Facts, limited to what is in the record.
6. Grievances, each tied to a lower-court consideration.
7. Evidence and file references.
8. Costs and ancillary requests.
9. Signature and enclosure list.

## Style Rules

- Write in a formal, restrained and impersonal tone.
- Prefer short procedural statements over narrative persuasion.
- Use numbered headings and subheadings.
- Address admissibility before merits.
- For constitutional grievances, state the right and explain the violation specifically.
- Avoid saying that a fact is proven unless the record support is identified.
- Avoid new evidence unless admissibility is verified.
- Distinguish `aktenkundig`, `bestritten`, `behauptet`, `nicht verifiziert` and `aus den Akten abgeleitet`.
- Tie every legal proposition to a verified statute or verified official decision.

## Useful German Formulations

Use cautiously and adapt to the record:

- `Der angefochtene Entscheid ist ein Endentscheid im Sinne von Art. 90 BGG.`
- `Die Beschwerde richtet sich gegen einen Entscheid einer letzten kantonalen Instanz.`
- `Die Beschwerdeschrift setzt sich gezielt mit den tragenden Erwägungen auseinander.`
- `Soweit der Sachverhalt gerügt wird, wird dargelegt, weshalb die Feststellung offensichtlich unrichtig ist.`
- `Die Rüge betrifft nicht eine blosse abweichende Würdigung, sondern einen entscheidwesentlichen Punkt.`

## French And Italian Use

French and Italian output may use the same source discipline and structural caution, but this run did not build a validated French or Italian style corpus. Mark confidence as LOW unless a separate corpus is verified.

## Abstention Rules

Abstain or downgrade confidence when:

- the source is not official;
- identity is not verified;
- only metadata is available;
- the dispositive or reasoning is incomplete;
- a citation is syntactically plausible but not resolved;
- the user asks for individual judge style without official authorship evidence;
- the requested filing depends on facts not in the record.

## Confidence Policy

- VERY_HIGH: only for direct metadata from official sources and low-risk structural observations.
- HIGH: repeated pattern across verified full-text decisions.
- MEDIUM: supported by a small verified corpus or adjacent comparator.
- LOW: limited analogy or non-German use.
- ABSTAIN_REQUIRED: unverifiable source, missing full text for legal proposition, or unsupported individual attribution.
