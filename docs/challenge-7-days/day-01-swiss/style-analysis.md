# Style Analysis

Status: PARTIAL

Corpus: 8 official full-text decisions of the II. civil-law division, German language, plus one I. civil-law comparator. The analysis describes institutional drafting style only.

## What Is Verified

Verified facts:

- The included decisions were resolved from official Swiss Federal Supreme Court pages.
- Each included decision has identity verified and full text verified by Swiss Legal MCP.
- The cited federal statutes were verified as official Fedlex records.
- Composition is verified where recorded in the official decision header.

Inferences:

- Repeated structure, sequencing and tone are inferred from the verified corpus.
- The corpus is too small to make statistical claims.
- No individual authorial style is identified.

## Observed Institutional Patterns

### 1. Fixed Judicial Architecture

The decisions generally follow a stable architecture: court identification, case number, date, division, composition, parties, matter, challenged decision, facts or procedural background, legal considerations, dispositive, costs and communication.

Support: all included decisions.

Confidence: HIGH for the verified corpus.

### 2. Admissibility Before Merits

The court typically tests jurisdiction, appeal type, finality, standing, value threshold, deadlines and motivation before substantive issues. This is visible in `5A_16/2007`, `5A_36/2007`, `5A_366/2016`, `5A_877/2014`, `5A_250/2023`, `5A_936/2022` and `5D_9/2020`.

Drafting consequence: a brief should not start with rhetorical merits. It should first make the procedural path easy to accept.

Confidence: HIGH.

### 3. Concise Fact Handling

Facts are usually presented in a compressed procedural chronology. The court distinguishes the lower decision, procedural steps, requests and federal filing. The facts are not narrated dramatically.

Support: `5A_16/2007`, `5A_36/2007`, `5A_388/2015`, `5A_936/2022`.

Confidence: HIGH.

### 4. Threshold Language For Deficient Complaints

Where submissions fail, the court often reasons through the complaint's lack of engagement with the lower-court reasoning, insufficient constitutional grievance, or mere presentation of the appellant's own facts. This pattern is especially visible in simplified non-entry decisions such as `5A_250/2023`, `5D_9/2020` and `5A_54/2014` as a limited excluded support.

Confidence: HIGH for procedural drafting patterns; MEDIUM for the broader corpus.

### 5. Numbered, Layered Legal Reasoning

The reasoning is modular. It moves from rule to consequence, often with nested numbers for doctrinal steps or subsumption. It avoids decorative transitions.

Support: `5A_366/2016`, `5A_877/2014`, `5A_936/2022`.

Confidence: HIGH.

### 6. Controlled Use Of Authorities

The court cites statutes, BGE references and prior judgments as supporting scaffolding. Authorities are tied to specific procedural or substantive propositions.

Support: `5A_386/2016`, `5A_366/2016`, `5A_877/2014`, `5D_9/2020`, `5A_936/2022`.

Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.

### 7. Formal, Impersonal Voice

The institutional voice is concise and impersonal. The decision reports what parties request, what the lower court held, what the legal standard requires and why the outcome follows.

Confidence: HIGH.

### 8. Costs As A Short Consequence

Costs are handled near the end as a direct consequence of outcome under the relevant BGG provisions. The reasoning is usually compact.

Support: all included decisions.

Confidence: HIGH.

## Negative Findings

- No reliable individual writing style can be extracted.
- No judge should be credited as the author merely because named in `Besetzung`.
- The corpus does not support French or Italian style replication beyond general Swiss Federal Supreme Court caution.
- BGE excerpts with incomplete dispositive should not drive drafting.

## Practical Drafting Profile

A brief aiming to fit this institutional style should:

- use numbered sections;
- identify the challenged decision early;
- establish jurisdiction and timeliness before merits;
- separate facts, admissibility and grievances;
- quote or cite only verified authorities;
- make each grievance target a specific consideration of the lower decision;
- avoid speculative facts and rhetorical escalation;
- request a precise dispositive result.
